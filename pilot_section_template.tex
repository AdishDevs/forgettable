\section{Pilot Study}
\label{sec:results}
The pilot runs the complete pipeline of Sections~\ref{sec:problem}--\ref{sec:setup} at reduced scale, to check that the benchmark produces reversion under retraining and to obtain first estimates for every method. Its results are exploratory: they do not test the registered hypotheses, which require the full study.

\subsection{Pilot Setup and Deviations from the Protocol}
\label{sec:pilot-setup}
The pilot uses @@NP@@ personas generated with one seed. Each persona has 22 sessions and 12 facts: four superseded facts (earlier value in one session, update in two sessions, so $r_f = 2$), two erase controls, two matched nonsuperseded controls stated in two sessions, and four unaffected facts. Each session yields three training examples (the statement and two question--answer pairs), and each fact has three held-out paraphrase probes. Adapters and references are trained once (one seed) with the values in Table~\ref{tab:hyper}, and the unlearning methods use those fixed values without a search. Because $\sigma_{\mathrm{floor}}$ needs several retrain seeds, the inclusion rule \eqref{eq:incl} is applied with a zero threshold: a superseded fact is included if the trained adapter prefers the update and retraining raises the probability of the earlier value. The oracle and the replay-only and uniform-replay ablations are not run. Utility is the share of unaffected and control facts for which the stored value is more probable than three distractor values, relative to the adapter before deletion.

\subsection{Cost}
\label{sec:pilot-cost}
Training one persona adapter took @@TTRAIN@@~s on average and retraining the reference took @@TRETRAIN@@~s, against about @@TUNL@@~s per unlearning run. At this scale an unlearning run therefore costs about @@COSTRATIO@@ of a retrain; the advantage of approximate unlearning grows with the length of a user's history, which the full study varies only through the persona size.
